**`mockbank.bai2.read` reads a real bank's BAI2 file** (#114). It refused any
file containing an `88`, and would have misread two other things if it had got
past that. All three came from one wrong assumption - that a record's width is a
constant - and all three are settled against
`tests/samples/external/bai2-sample2.txt`, vendored from
[moov-io/bai2](https://github.com/moov-io/bai2) at the same pinned commit as
`sample1.txt`.

**A continuation continues a field stream, not a record.** `88` was not declared
at all, and declaring it with fields of its own would have been wrong too: in
`sample2.txt` a summary group splits straight across the boundary, its type code
ending the `03` and its amount beginning the `88`.

```
03,0975312468,,010,500000,,,190,70000000,4,0,110/
88,70000000,15,D,3,0,20000000,1,30000000,3,20000000/
```

So a continuation is folded into the record above before anything is parsed, and
nothing further down has to know continuations exist. It may follow any record,
and it chains - 68 deep in one of moov's samples.

**A funds type carries its own width.** One field for blank, `Z` or a digit;
three for `V`, which is followed by an availability date and time; four for `S`,
followed by three availability amounts; and 2 + 2n for `D`, followed by a count
and that many (days, amount) pairs. The old reader assumed one field always,
which made a `16` two fields too wide to accept, and - worse - made a summary
group pass the "repeats in fours" check whenever a `V` group appeared in pairs
and then read every amount out of the wrong slot, raising a bare `ValueError`
from `_amounts_in` rather than refusing. `statements` was reading a value-dated
entry's availability date as its bank reference for the same reason.

**An amount may be signed or bare.** Across moov-io/bai2's samples 45 control
totals are written bare and 7 signed, and one record of `sample2.txt` has
`010,+4350000` beside `040,2830000`. This corrects something #57 recorded: that
sample settled *a* notation, not the notation. The mock still signs what it
writes, because that is self-consistent, but it is a choice rather than the
format's requirement and the module now says so.

**And the two numbers a trailer states are counted over different units.** A
record count counts physical records, continuations included; a control total
sums the logical record. Both are right in every file this mock writes, and only
a file with an `88` in it can tell them apart.

The check worth naming is not that the files read. It is that `trailers_agree`
finds nothing in either of them: every control total and every record count,
recomputed from the records it covers. A wrong fold or a wrong funds-type width
changes one of those numbers.

Three things it still does not read, each pinned by a passing test and needing a
sample this project does not hold yet: a `16` whose text field contains commas
and runs to the end of the record, several records packed onto one line separated
by `/`, and a record with no `/` at all where the newline terminates.
